This is the most cliché post ever, but GitHub warned me today for being inactive. I thought I didn't have any brain cells left to write anything at the end of the semester, so I'm gonna recommend the famous epidemiology textbook _Causal Inference What If_.

Generally, the sequence of the book should be _Gordis Epidemiology_, _Modern Epidemiology_, _What If_. When you have accumulated enough fundamental and advanced epidemiological knowledge, reading _What If_ can help you go over everything under the counterfactual framework. The book has three sections: Causal inference without models, Causal inference with models, and Causal inference for time-varying treatments. You will revisit the epidemiology concepts in section 1, learn more about advanced confounding adjustment in section 2, and time-varying treatment modeling in section 3. This also includes two relatively new topics: target trial emulation and causal mediation analysis. In the future, some cutting-edge topics like causal deep learning and transportability should also be included. Basically, this book includes most of the topics in Harvard faction causal inference of epidemiology. 

Other than the comprehensive coverage of topics, the book is also clear and easy to read. I've read 70% of the book while taking EPI289 and EPI207. One of the biggest feelings is that it helps me to solve many confusions from the class and taking exam of 289 and 207. If you are in a hurry to learn how to do analysis, the book also includes R and SAS code for you to use. Also, the book is completely free, so you can access and download it [here](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook) today.
